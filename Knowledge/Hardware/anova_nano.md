# Anova Nano

Immersion circulator (sous vide), chosen instead of a yogurt maker. Holds water at an exact temperature in any pot, for as long as needed.

**Why this one**

- ±0.1 °C accuracy and a wide range (0 to 92 °C, per the thread), versus ±0.5 to 2 °C on yogurt makers.
- Clips onto any pot: 1.5 L today, a stockpot tomorrow. No capacity limit.
- One machine, many jobs: temperature_controlled_fermentation, tea_and_coffee_water_temperature, sous_vide_vs_bain_marie, layered_stock.

**Purchase decision**

- Chosen: Nano at R$ 558. Original budget for the project: up to R$ 600.
- Dropped: Nano 3.0 (R$ 1000; the difference is faster heat-up and app features, not accuracy), Precision Cooker Pro in a bundle with a vacuum sealer (R$ 7500; overkill for 1.5 to 2 L pots). A standalone sealer runs R$ 150 to R$ 300 if sous vide meat ever becomes a thing.
- All Anova units are imported and pricey. Buy from an official distributor or "sold and shipped by Amazon" to avoid grey-market resellers.

**Alternatives passed on**

- Izumi: fixed 42 °C, no timer.
- TopTherm Greco (2 L): built for yogurt (42 to 46 °C), 6 h default.
- Generic digital "36 h" yogurt makers (R$ 190 to R$ 380): same factory, different stickers, no quality guarantee.
- Brod & Taylor Folding Proofer: 120 V and far too big (fits 7.5 L of yogurt).
